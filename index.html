About

I'm a researcher, strategist, and educator whose career has been defined by one consistent challenge: taking things that are genuinely complex — health equity, behavior change, institutional learning, cultural difference — and making them legible, actionable, and human.

My PhD in medical humanities gave me rigorous training in qualitative research, critical theory, and the politics of health communication. I spent five years conducting in-depth interviews on HIV/AIDS risk perception in France, publishing in peer-reviewed journals, and building international research networks. That grounding informs everything I do.

But I've never been just an academic. Alongside the research I was designing professional development, leading technology adoption, managing international programs, and coaching educators at scale. I've secured grants, built tools from scratch, and delivered outcomes that show up in the data — a 19% improvement in DEI belonging scores, 200+ faculty trained on AI integration, a program scaled from one campus to nine.

I'm drawn to organizations where thinking matters — where research informs strategy, content has stakes, and the people around the table are genuinely trying to solve hard problems. That's led me to health communications, learning strategy, and program management roles across academic, K–12, nonprofit, and publishing contexts.

Currently exploring opportunities in health communications, public health strategy, program leadership, and research-driven content — particularly in health equity, LGBTQ+ health, and education/edtech spaces. Based in Miami; open to remote and hybrid roles.
